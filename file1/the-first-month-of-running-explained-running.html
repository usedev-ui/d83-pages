The First Month of Running, Explained — Running

Progress in running comes from consistency, not from intensity in any single session.

By Daily Ledger Desk November 12, 2026 2 min read

A run you can finish comfortably is worth more than an ambitious one you cannot repeat.

The aim here is a clear starting point: what to do first, what to skip, and how to tell whether it is working.

Why it matters

It is worth understanding why these habits work before trying to follow them, because the reasoning is what makes them stick.

Running at a pace where you can hold a conversation keeps effort aerobic and reduces injury risk.

Connective tissue adapts more slowly than muscle, so easy early weeks protect joints while strength catches up.

Adding distance or intensity too quickly is the standard route to the overuse injuries that stop beginners entirely.

What to expect

This is the part that tends to surprise people: the day to day version is straightforward.

Soreness that settles within a day or two is normal, while sharp or localised pain is a signal to stop.

Expect the first few weeks to feel more like brisk walking with running intervals, which is exactly right.

The short version:

  • Start smaller than feels impressive, then add to it only once it is easy.
  • Track one simple measure so you can tell whether a change is actually working.
  • Review after a couple of weeks and keep only what earned its place.
  • Consistency matters more than intensity in almost every case.

Step by step

Follow these in order. Each step assumes the one before it, so there is little value in jumping ahead.

  1. Get a pair of properly fitted running shoes and comfortable clothing.
  2. Start with a session of alternating one minute running and two minutes walking, repeated a few times.
  3. Run three times a week with rest days between, and walk on the other days.
  4. Gradually lengthen the running intervals while keeping the same easy effort.
  5. Add a longer session once a week only after the shorter ones feel manageable.

Practical tips

  • Warm up with a few minutes of walking before the first run interval.
  • Wear shoes suited to running and replace them once the cushioning feels flat.
  • Keep the easy pace slow enough to hold a conversation comfortably.
  • Alternate running and walking rather than trying to run continuously from day one.
  • Track sessions by time rather than distance in the first month.

Quick answers

Is it fine to walk during a run?

Yes, and for beginners it is preferable to stopping entirely. Walk breaks let you cover more time with less impact and recover faster.

How soon should I see progress?

Most beginners notice a difference within a few weeks, mainly because the body becomes more efficient at the movement before any large fitness change occurs.

What if my knees hurt?

Sharp or persistent pain means stop and reduce load. Usually it comes from increasing too quickly, though persistent pain deserves a professional assessment.

Should I stretch before running?

A short walk or easy movement works better as a warm up than long static stretches. Save extended stretching for after the run.

Treat this as a starting point rather than a rulebook. Adjust anything that does not fit your circumstances, and keep the parts that clearly help.
